Electric Fields & Electric Potential

Explore field direction, field strength, voltage, uniform fields, and equipotential maps through visual simulations and guided challenges.

Start Here: Big Ideas

Electric fields describe how a source charge affects the space around it. Electric potential describes energy per unit charge. Use this app to connect the diagrams, equations, and calculations.

Electric Field
E = F / q

Field strength is force per unit charge. Unit: N/C.

Point Charge Field
E = k|Q| / r²

Field strength decreases with distance squared.

Electric Potential
V = W / q

Voltage is energy transferred per coulomb. Unit: V = J/C.

Point Charge Potential
V = kQ / r

Potential can be + or −. Use the sign of Q.

Uniform Field
E = ΔV / d

Between parallel plates, E is nearly constant. Unit: V/m.

Constant: k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². Always convert cm → m and μC → C before substituting.

1. Field Direction

+
Positive source charge
Field lines point outward.
−
Negative source charge
Field lines point inward.

2. Field Lines

Electric field lines show the direction of force on a positive test charge.

Closer field lines mean a stronger electric field.

3. Potential

Electric potential tells how much energy each coulomb has because of its position in an electric field.

Voltage is not energy. Voltage is energy per unit charge.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Do not define field direction using a negative test charge.
  • Do not draw field lines crossing each other.
  • Do not confuse electric potential V with potential energy U.
  • Do not forget to convert cm to m and μC to C in calculations.

Guided Challenge Problems

Challenge score: 0/5

Challenge 1 E = F/q

A 3.0 μC test charge experiences a 0.018 N force. Calculate the electric field strength.

Convert: q = 3.0 × 10⁻⁶ C.
E = F/q = 0.018 ÷ (3.0 × 10⁻⁶) = 6.0 × 10³ N/C.

Challenge 2 E = ΔV/d

Two plates have a potential difference of 240 V and are separated by 0.060 m. Find the uniform field.

E = ΔV/d = 240 ÷ 0.060 = 4.0 × 10³ V/m. This is also 4.0 × 10³ N/C.

Challenge 3 V = W/q

Moving a charge requires 0.045 J of energy. If the charge is 5.0 mC, calculate the potential difference.

Convert: q = 5.0 × 10⁻³ C.
V = W/q = 0.045 ÷ 0.0050 = 9.0 V.

Challenge 4 V = kQ/r

Calculate the electric potential 0.30 m from a +2.0 μC point charge. (k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²)

V = kQ/r = (8.99 × 10⁹)(2.0 × 10⁻⁶) ÷ 0.30 ≈ 6.0 × 10⁴ V.
A positive source charge gives a positive potential.

Challenge 5 ΔU = qΔV

A +4.0 μC charge moves to a point where the potential is 150 V lower. Find the change in its potential energy, and state what happens to its speed if no other forces act.

ΔU = qΔV = (4.0 × 10⁻⁶)(−150) = −6.0 × 10⁻⁴ J.
Potential energy decreases, so kinetic energy increases — the charge speeds up.

Integrated Extension

A positive charge moves from high potential to low potential between two plates. Use the field direction, force direction, and energy ideas to explain what happens.

Sample answer: The electric field points from the positive plate/high potential side toward the negative plate/low potential side. A positive charge feels a force in the same direction as the field, so it accelerates toward lower potential. Its electric potential energy decreases, and that energy can become kinetic energy.
